# UX laws

A finding may name a law only alongside a rule id. The rule is the decision; the law is the reason it holds.

| Law | In one line | Backs |
| --- | ----------- | ----- |
| Hick's law | more choices take longer to decide | colour-one-primary, layout-fits-1280 |
| Law of proximity | things close together read as a group | layout-no-card-in-card, edges-one-hairline |
| Law of common region | a boundary groups what it holds, so use few | layout-no-card-in-card |
| Fitts's law | small, distant targets are slower to hit | icons-sized-by-role, controls-icon-button-label |
| Miller's law | people hold a few items in working memory | disclosure-summary-first |
| Aesthetic-usability effect | tidy screens feel easier to use | edges-borders-visible, layout-spacing-scale |
| Jakob's law | people expect your app to work like others they use | controls-icon-button-label |
| Tesler's law | some complexity cannot be removed, only moved | disclosure-summary-first, copy-say-it-once |
